Telecom Customer Churn Analysis & Prediction Dashboard

This project analyzes 6,418 telecommunications customer records to identify customer churn patterns and predict potential future churn risk. The analysis covers customer demographics, geographic distribution, contract and payment structures, tenure, service usage, financial metrics, and reported churn reasons.

The project follows an end-to-end data analytics workflow, beginning with data ingestion and transformation in Microsoft SQL Server, followed by data preparation and interactive visualization in Microsoft Power BI. A Random Forest classification model was developed in Python to predict potential churn among new joiners, while SHAP analysis was used to interpret the most influential features contributing to model predictions.

The findings were transformed into interactive Power BI dashboards that provide an overview of historical churn performance and model-identified potential churn-risk customers. The project demonstrates how data engineering, business intelligence, and machine learning can be integrated to support proactive customer retention and data-driven decision-making.

Telecommunications companies operate in highly competitive markets where customer retention is critical for maintaining long-term revenue and business growth. However, when customer data is stored in raw or fragmented formats, it can be difficult to identify the customer characteristics and business conditions associated with churn.

Without a centralized and interactive reporting system, business teams may struggle to identify high-churn customer segments, understand the relationship between contract structures and customer attrition, evaluate regional and service-related churn patterns, and determine the primary reasons customers leave.

Furthermore, relying solely on historical churn analysis can result in a reactive approach, where retention efforts are initiated only after customers have already discontinued their services. This project addresses the challenge by integrating SQL Server-based ETL, Power BI analytics, and machine learning to analyze historical churn patterns and proactively identify new customers with potential churn risk.

  • Develop an end-to-end ETL workflow using Microsoft SQL Server to load, clean, standardize, and structure customer data.
  • Create analytical SQL views to separate historical churn data from new-joiner data used for prediction.
  • Develop an interactive Power BI dashboard to analyze customer churn across demographic, geographic, account, payment, service, and churn-reason dimensions.
  • Identify customer segments and business conditions associated with higher churn rates.
  • Develop a Random Forest classification model to predict potential customer churn based on historical customer characteristics.
  • Apply the trained model to new joiners to identify potential churn-risk customers.
  • Use SHAP analysis to interpret the most influential features contributing to model predictions.
  • Provide data-driven insights and recommendations to support proactive customer retention strategies.

The dataset consists of 6,418 telecommunications customer records covering demographic, geographic, account, payment, service, financial, and customer status information. Customers were classified as Stayed, Churned, or Joined. Historical Stayed and Churned customers were used for churn analysis and Random Forest model training, while the trained model was applied to 411 Joined customers to identify potential churn-risk profiles.

This project followed an end-to-end data analytics workflow using Microsoft SQL Server, Power BI, Python, Scikit-learn, SHAP, and Matplotlib. Raw customer data was first ingested and cleaned in SQL Server through data quality assessment, NULL value handling, standardization, and analytical view creation.

The prepared data was then transformed in Power Query and visualized through interactive Power BI dashboards to analyze customer churn patterns and potential churn-risk profiles.

For predictive analysis, Python and Scikit-learn were used to develop a Random Forest classification model. Model performance was evaluated using a confusion matrix, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The trained model was then applied to new joiners to identify potential churn-risk customers.

Finally, SHAP and Matplotlib were used to interpret model predictions and identify the features with the greatest influence on churn risk. The analysis combined ETL, data transformation, KPI analysis, descriptive analysis, segmentation analysis, classification modeling, and model interpretation to transform raw customer data into actionable insights for customer retention.

1. Contract Structure Is the Strongest Churn Indicator

Month-to-Month customers recorded the highest churn rate at 46.53%, compared with 11.04% for One Year contracts and only 2.73% for Two Year contracts. Contract type was also identified as the most influential feature in the Random Forest model, highlighting long-term contract conversion as a key retention opportunity.

2. Churn Risk Varies Across Customer Lifecycle, Demographics, and Payment Methods

Customers aged over 50 recorded the highest churn rate at 31.55%. Churn was also relatively elevated among customers with less than six months of tenure at 26.37% and those within the 18–24 month tenure period at 27.24%. Mailed Check users recorded the highest churn rate among payment methods at 37.82%, compared with 14.80% among Credit Card users.

3. Regional and Service Performance Show Significant Churn Variation

Jammu & Kashmir recorded the highest regional churn rate at 57.19%, followed by Assam at 38.13% and Jharkhand at 34.51%. Fiber Optic customers also recorded a relatively high churn rate of 41.10%, indicating that specific regions and service segments should be further investigated.

4. Competitor Pressure Is the Leading Recorded Churn Category

Competitor-related reasons accounted for 761 churned customers, representing approximately 43.9% of total churn. This exceeded other recorded categories and highlights competitive pressure as a major factor associated with customer attrition.

5. Predictive Modeling Identified a High-Risk Customer Segment

The Random Forest model achieved 82.0% accuracy, with 63.7% recall for churned customers. When applied to 411 new joiners, the model identified 315 potential churn-risk profiles, of which 314 were Month-to-Month customers. This provides a prioritized customer population for proactive retention initiatives.

The analysis of 6,418 customers identified a significant overall churn rate of 27.0%, with customer attrition strongly associated with contract structure, customer lifecycle stage, payment method, geographic location, and service type.

Month-to-Month customers represented the highest-risk contract segment, while competitor-related reasons represented the largest recorded churn category. Regional and service-level variations also highlighted specific areas requiring further investigation, particularly Jammu & Kashmir and Fiber Optic customers.

The Random Forest model achieved 82.0% accuracy and 63.7% recall for churned customers. When applied to 411 new joiners, the model identified 315 potential churn-risk profiles, with 314 using Month-to-Month contracts. These results demonstrate how predictive analytics can complement historical analysis by supporting proactive and targeted customer retention strategies.

  • Prioritize Month-to-Month customers for targeted contract conversion campaigns by offering billing credits, loyalty benefits, or additional service benefits for One-Year and Two-Year contract upgrades.
  • Use the 315 model-identified potential churn-risk profiles as a prioritized retention list for proactive customer outreach, satisfaction checks, service issue investigation, and targeted retention offers.
  • Develop lifecycle-based retention strategies by focusing on onboarding and early engagement for customers with less than six months of tenure, while implementing renewal and loyalty initiatives for customers approaching the 18–24 month tenure period.
  • Conduct regional investigations in high-churn areas, particularly Jammu & Kashmir, Assam, Jharkhand, and Chhattisgarh, to evaluate potential differences in competition, service quality, pricing, and customer experience.
  • Review the Fiber Optic customer experience by analyzing service reliability, pricing competitiveness, technical support performance, and customer complaints.
  • Improve payment engagement and convenience among customers using Mailed Check and Bank Withdrawal methods by promoting simpler digital payment options.
  • Develop competitive retention strategies in response to the high proportion of churn associated with competitor-related reasons, including competitive pricing reviews, service improvements, loyalty programs, and targeted offers.
  • Continue improving the churn prediction model through hyperparameter tuning, model comparison, cross-validation, classification threshold optimization, and ongoing performance monitoring using future customer outcomes.
