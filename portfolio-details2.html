Adventure Works Sales Analysis Dashboard

This project analyzes historical Adventure Works sales data to evaluate financial performance, product profitability, customer demographics, and geographic sales distribution. Raw transactional data was transformed into actionable business insights through an interactive Microsoft Excel Business Intelligence dashboard.

The solution integrates Power Query for data preparation, Power Pivot for relational data modeling, and DAX for dynamic calculations. The dashboard enables users to monitor KPIs, explore performance trends, compare segments, and support data-driven business decisions.

Adventure Works' historical sales data contains valuable information across products, customers, territories, and time. However, analyzing fragmented transactional data can make it difficult to quickly identify key profitability drivers and performance patterns.

This project addresses the challenge by consolidating the data into a relational model and interactive dashboard, enabling efficient analysis of financial performance, product contribution, customer demographics, and geographic markets.

  • Build an integrated relational data model and interactive sales dashboard using Microsoft Excel.
  • Analyze revenue, COGS, total profit, and profit margin across time periods.
  • Identify the most profitable products, pricing segments, customer demographics, and geographic markets.
  • Evaluate product portfolio performance, including sold and unsold product variations.
  • Translate analytical findings into actionable business recommendations.

The project uses the Adventure Works sales database, modeled using a star schema that connects the FactInternetSales table with product, customer, territory, and date dimensions.

The project followed an end-to-end data analytics workflow using Microsoft Excel. Power Query was used to extract, clean, transform, and prepare the Adventure Works sales data before loading it into Power Pivot. A relational data model based on a star schema was then developed by connecting the sales fact table with relevant product, customer, territory, and date dimensions.

DAX was used to create dynamic financial and profitability calculations, while Pivot Tables and Pivot Charts supported the analysis and visualization of sales performance across different time periods, products, customer demographics, and geographic markets. Interactive Slicers were added to enable dynamic filtering and cross-analysis by year, geography, and product category.

1. Annual and Quarterly Profitability Performance

Profitability varied across different time periods, with 2007 recording the highest annual total profit at approximately $43M. At the quarterly level, Q2 was the strongest-performing quarter, generating $39.02M in total profit. This indicates that the second quarter represented a particularly important period for the company's overall profitability.

2. Monthly and Weekly Profitability Patterns

Monthly profitability showed distinct seasonal fluctuations, with May, June, and December recording the highest profit levels. In addition, weekdays contributed 72% of total profit, with Thursday and Friday recording the highest transaction activity. These patterns suggest that both seasonal periods and specific weekdays played an important role in overall sales performance.

3. Product Profitability Concentration

Profitability was highly concentrated among a small number of products. The Top 5 Products by Total Profit contributed 24.8% of total profit, primarily driven by the Mountain-200 Black and Silver variants. Black and Red product variants also generated the highest total profit, indicating stronger profitability associated with these product characteristics.

4. Premium Product Segment Contribution

Products priced above $150 contributed 95.4% of total profit, highlighting the dominant role of the premium product segment in Adventure Works' profitability. This indicates that high-priced products were the primary contributors to financial performance and that the company's profit structure was heavily dependent on premium offerings.

5. Customer Demographic Profitability

Customers aged 50 and above contributed 56.3% of total profit, making them the largest contributing age group. Profit contribution by gender was relatively balanced, with Female customers contributing 50.4% and Male customers contributing 49.6% of total profit. This suggests that age was a more pronounced differentiating factor in profit contribution than gender.

6. High-Value Customer Contribution

The analysis identified Willie Xu and Jordan Turner among the highest-profit customers. These high-value customers represent potential opportunities for targeted retention and relationship-building strategies to maintain their contribution to overall profitability.

7. Geographic Profit Concentration

Profitability was concentrated in a limited number of geographic markets. The United States and Australia collectively contributed 62.7% of total profit, making them the primary profit-contributing markets. While these markets represent strong business performance, their significant contribution also highlights the importance of geographic diversification to reduce dependence on a limited number of markets.

Adventure Works demonstrated strong overall profitability, generating $126.29M in total profit from $307.09M in revenue, with a 41.1% profit margin. The analysis identified several key profitability drivers: premium products priced above $150, customers aged 50+, and the United States and Australia markets. Temporal analysis also revealed strong performance during Q2, May, June, December, and weekdays, particularly Thursday and Friday. However, the concentration of profit across specific product, customer, and geographic segments also highlights opportunities to improve portfolio and market diversification.

  • Ensure the availability of high-performing products before Q2 and December, while strategically scheduling campaigns around high-activity periods such as Thursday and Friday.
  • Leverage customer purchasing patterns to develop more relevant retention, loyalty, and marketing initiatives for the segment contributing the largest share of total profit.
  • Evaluate the 448 unsold product variations through bundling, cross-selling, targeted promotions, or portfolio rationalization to improve product utilization.
  • Expand performance in secondary markets such as the United Kingdom, Germany, France, and Canada to reduce dependence on the United States and Australia.
