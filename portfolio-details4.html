Sales & Marketing Call Center Performance Dashboard

This project analyzes 200,000 customer call records collected over a one-year period from January to December to evaluate Sales & Marketing Call Center performance across operational, sales, customer, and geographic dimensions.

The analysis focuses on call volume trends, agent workload distribution, call outcomes, abandoned call drivers, product performance, customer demographics, and geographic distribution. The findings were transformed into an interactive Microsoft Power BI dashboard that enables users to monitor key performance indicators, explore business patterns, identify operational bottlenecks, and support data-driven decision-making.

Managing a large volume of call center data can make it difficult for management to quickly evaluate operational performance and identify important business patterns.

With 200,000 raw call records, key questions related to agent workload, monthly call trends, abandoned call reasons, product performance, customer demographics, and geographic distribution may be difficult to answer efficiently when the data is presented only in its raw form.

Without a centralized and interactive reporting system, performance monitoring can become time-consuming and may delay the identification of operational problems and business opportunities.

This project addresses the challenge by transforming raw call logs into an interactive Power BI dashboard that centralizes key performance indicators and enables management to analyze call center performance across multiple business dimensions.

  • Develop an interactive dashboard to centralize and visualize Sales & Marketing Call Center performance.
  • Analyze monthly call volume trends and time-of-day call patterns.
  • Evaluate agent workload distribution and call-handling contribution.
  • Identify the primary reasons behind abandoned calls.
  • Analyze call outcome performance and status distribution.
  • Evaluate product performance based on call activity and successful outcomes.
  • Examine customer demographic patterns, particularly gender distribution.
  • Analyze geographic call distribution across states.
  • Provide data-driven insights to support operational evaluation and business decision-making.

The dataset consists of 200,000 customer call records collected from January to December. Each record represents a call interaction and contains information related to the call, assigned agent, customer, product, outcome, and geographic location.

Variable Description
Call_ID Unique identifier for each call record.
Date Date on which the call was recorded and used for time-based analysis.
Agent_First_Name First name of the call center agent handling the call.
Agent_Last_Name Last name of the call center agent handling the call.
Agent_Rating Customer rating given to the agent on a scale of 1 to 5.
Product_Discussed Product discussed during the call, including Loans, Internet Package, Electronics, Travel Package, and Insurance.
Call_Duration_Minutes Duration of the call measured in minutes.
Call_Outcome Final outcome of the call, including Success, Failure, or Abandoned.
Customer_Age Age of the customer associated with the call.
Callers_Name Name of the customer or caller.
Customer_Gender Gender of the customer, categorized as Male or Female.
State State associated with the customer's location.
Customer_Income_Bracket Customer income classification: High, Medium, or Low.
Time_of_Day Time segment when the call occurred: Morning, Afternoon, or Evening.
Follow_Up_Call_Required Indicates whether a follow-up call is required.
Repeat_Customer Indicates whether the customer is a repeat customer.
Reason_Call_Abandoned Reason associated with an abandoned call, including Long Wait Time, Agent Not Available, Technical Issues, and Customer Hung Up.

The project was developed using Microsoft Power BI as the primary platform for data transformation, analysis, and interactive dashboard development.

The call log dataset was prepared and structured to support analysis across multiple business dimensions, including call outcomes, agents, products, time, customer demographics, and geographic locations. DAX measures were created to calculate key performance indicators and support analytical calculations for call volume, call outcomes, customer ratings, and other performance metrics.

The dashboard uses a combination of KPI cards, donut charts, bar charts, line charts, and geographic visualizations to present performance patterns in a clear and interactive format. Slicers and filters were also implemented to allow users to dynamically explore the data based on selected time periods and call-related dimensions.

The analysis combines KPI analysis, time-based trend analysis, agent workload analysis, abandoned call analysis, product performance analysis, customer demographic analysis, and geographic analysis to transform raw call logs into actionable business insights.

1. Call Outcome Performance

The dashboard shows that the Success status group accounted for 58% of calls, while the remaining 42% were classified as Others based on the status-grouping logic applied in the analysis. This indicates a substantial proportion of successful call outcomes while still leaving opportunities to improve overall call effectiveness and operational performance.

2. Monthly Call Volume Trends

Call volume remained relatively stable throughout the year, with July recording the highest monthly volume at 17,172 calls, while February recorded the lowest at 15,856 calls. The moderate variation in monthly activity provides a useful basis for workforce planning, staffing allocation, and resource management throughout the year.

3. Agent Workload Distribution

The Top 5 agents handled 69% of total call volume, indicating a significant concentration of workload among a small group of agents. Ava Sandoval handled the highest call volume with 40,000 calls, followed by Elijah Hawkins with 32,000 calls and Drew Clay with 28,000 calls. This concentration highlights the importance of monitoring workload distribution and evaluating agent performance using multiple performance indicators rather than call volume alone.

4. Abandoned Call Drivers

Long Wait Time was the dominant reason for abandoned calls, accounting for approximately 94.4% of all abandoned calls. The significant concentration of abandoned calls under this category indicates that customer waiting time and queue management represent the most critical operational bottlenecks in the call center.

5. Product Performance

Internet Package and Loans generated the highest successful call volumes, with 23,277 and 23,088 successful calls, respectively. Their strong performance makes them important products for future upselling, cross-selling, and bundling strategies, while lower-performing products may require further evaluation to identify opportunities for improvement.

6. Customer Demographic Distribution

Male customers accounted for 70% of total calls, while female customers represented 30%. This indicates a significant difference in customer representation within the call records. Further analysis of product preferences and success rates by gender could help identify opportunities for more targeted customer engagement strategies.

7. Geographic Distribution

Call activity was concentrated across several key states, with New York recording the highest volume at 32,084 calls, followed by California and North Carolina. The Top 5 states accounted for 58% of total call volume, indicating a significant concentration of customer activity across several key geographic markets.

  • The call center handled a substantial volume of customer interactions, with the Success status group representing 58% of calls based on the analytical status-grouping logic applied in the dashboard.
  • Long Wait Time was the most significant operational bottleneck, accounting for approximately 94.4% of abandoned calls.
  • Call workload was highly concentrated among the Top 5 agents, who handled 69% of total call volume.
  • Internet Package and Loans emerged as the strongest-performing products based on successful call volume.
  • Customer activity was concentrated among male customers and several key states, creating opportunities for more targeted customer segmentation and regional marketing strategies.
  • The Power BI dashboard provides a centralized view of call center performance and enables management to monitor operational trends and identify potential areas for improvement.

  • Optimize Queue Management: Adjust agent scheduling and staffing levels during high-volume periods and consider implementing automated callback solutions to reduce customer waiting time and abandoned calls.
  • Improve Workload Distribution: Monitor workload concentration across agents and use performance benchmarking, coaching, and resource allocation strategies to support a more balanced distribution of call volume.
  • Strengthen Performance Monitoring: Evaluate agents using multiple performance indicators, including call volume, call outcomes, customer ratings, and call duration, rather than relying on call volume alone.
  • Develop Targeted Marketing Strategies: Use customer demographic and geographic patterns to develop more targeted marketing campaigns and investigate opportunities to improve engagement across underrepresented customer segments.
  • Leverage High-Performing Products: Utilize the strong performance of Internet Package and Loans through upselling, cross-selling, and product bundling strategies.
  • Conduct Further Product Analysis: Investigate the performance of lower-volume products to identify potential barriers to customer interest, sales conversion, or product positioning.
